Teardown Index

Who makes the foreign electronics in Russia's weapons

Which countries' firms make the foreign electronics inside Russia's weapons? This index scores every country by its share of the parts Ukrainian intelligence has recovered, weighted by how critical each part is, counted once per weapon type and traced to the parent company that owns the brand.

Methodology and sources

  • Base data. All — components in GUR's War & Sanctions database as of 30 Oct 2026, duplicates removed. GUR is the intelligence service of a country at war; check individual items before citing them.
  • Weapon families. Airframes and missiles of one model are merged into — families and each part counts once per family, leaving — entries.
  • Weighting. Tiers follow the Common High Priority List: — integrated circuits, — navigation and radio modules, — discrete and passive parts, — other. Tiers come from GUR's part names by keyword, so a few are misfiled.
  • Range. 1,000 resamples of the weapon families give the 90% range.
  • Dates. GUR dates — parts; year-week codes read from markings raise that to —. GUR's own dates alone give — made from 2022, because it tends to publish dates on newer parts.
  • Size adjustment. 2021 revenue or the nearest fiscal year from company releases: Analog Devices plus Maxim, Xilinx before AMD bought it, Intel's programmable-chip unit for Altera; euro and franc converted at 1.19 $/€ and 1.094 $/CHF. Total revenue is a rough stand-in for market share in the parts Russia buys.
  • Not yet included. A firm-by-firm match against CAR iTrace and RUSI's full company tables. This measures brand exposure in recovered weapons, not trade volumes or who shipped the parts.
